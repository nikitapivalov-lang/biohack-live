- [Studarium: epithelium](https://studarium.ru/article/77), [connective tissues](https://studarium.ru/article/78), [muscle](https://studarium.ru/article/79), [nervous tissue](https://studarium.ru/article/80).
- [Reactome: Smooth Muscle Contraction](https://www.reactome.org/content/detail/R-HSA-445355) and [Physiology, Smooth Muscle](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK526125/) for the optional calcium–calmodulin–MLCK pathway.

The primary lesson stays at EGE level. Molecular regulation, germ-layer exceptions and myelin details are in closed-by-default «Углублённо» sections. The standalone anatomy lesson «Ткани человека» now contains main, epithelium-simple, epithelium-stratified, epithelium-glandular, connective, atlas, skeletal and smooth. Ontogenesis links to it; legacy ontogenesis tissue routes redirect to their canonical standalone routes. The separate epithelial screens use original vector illustrations for six single-layer variants, three common stratified epithelia, two rarer stratified types in optional depth, and a separate glandular-function illustration. Classification, locations and functions were checked against Kuznetsov/Mushkambarov, sections 7.2–7.4 (especially pp.123–127). Ciliation and brush borders are specializations rather than new layer classes.

## Connective tissue atlas

New standalone screen: `#lesson/tissues/connective`. Thirteen illustrated adult tissue types/variants are arranged in five groups. Embryonic and rare specialized tissues are outside this EGE-oriented atlas. Compact and spongy are explicitly described as organizations of bone, not separate tissue families. Basic information is synthesized from Kuznetsov/Mushkambarov, 2016, topics 8–10 (pp.133–181); OpenStax section 4.3 was used for cross-checking. The diagrams are original BioHack SVGs and adapt to dark mode; photographs preserve their staining and full composition.

Twelve photographs were added; the existing white-adipose photograph is reused. Selected images use Commons preview sizes (500–1400 px wide), re-encoded to JPEG without cropping. Brown-adipose has a smaller original resolution. The spongy-bone image retains both ordinary and polarized-light panels and its original scale bar. Screen zoom is not a calibrated microscope magnification.

The lymph card deliberately uses a **normal lymph-node preparation**, not a supposed lymph smear. Its title, caption, recognition notes and modal identify the organ, while a separate original diagram explains lymph inside a vessel. The distinction between tissue, cell and organ is explicit.

## Revised overview drawings

The connective overview now depicts nine fibroblasts, two macrophages, bundled collagen fibrils and thinner branching elastic fibers within ground substance. The detailed loose-connective scheme uses the same richer original illustration with independent clip IDs; dense regular and irregular schemes each show eight distinct spindle-shaped fibroblasts. The nervous overview shows a branching multipolar neuron, a segmented myelinated axon, terminals, two astrocytes and a capillary. Astrocytic branches contact portions of the soma/dendrites and vessel; they are not depicted as a continuous myelin sheath. Labels and explanatory cards distinguish astrocytes from oligodendrocytes and Schwann cells. All drawings are original SVGs with reversible dark-theme colors.

Anatomical cross-check: [Neuroglial Cells, Neuroscience (Purves et al.)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK10869/), [The Astrocyte: Powerhouse and Recycling Center](https://cshperspectives.cshlp.org/content/7/12/a020396.full).

## Muscle morphology drawings (2026-10-25)

Original SVGs in the existing tissue palette; morphological reference: S. L. Kuznetsov, N. N. ## Interactive skeletal muscle dissection (2026-10-25)

Interaction reference: https://lab.neofamily.ru/muscle-contraction/ (progressive exploration from whole muscle to contractile structures). All BioHack drawings and implementation are original. Starts directly with an isolated muscle. Six levels: muscle, transverse cut, fascicle, multinucleated muscle fiber, myofibril, sarcomere. Connective sheaths and hierarchy follow Kuznetsov & Mushkambarov (2016), pp. 184–190, especially 186 (endo-/peri-/epimysium), 188–190 (sarcomeres, actin/myosin, sliding filaments and ATP-dependent cross-bridge cycle). Filament lengths and A-band stay constant during the shortened teaching cycle; relaxation shows recovery of length under external forces, not active pushing by filaments. Drawings are schematic, not to scale.

## Sarcomere clarity revision (2026-10-25)

## ATP-linked cross-bridge cycle and myosin detail (2026-10-25)

The existing sarcomere scene is 78% of its previous desktop width. On narrow displays the drawing retains a readable internal width with local horizontal panning; the page itself fits 390 px. The thick filament is drawn as ten intertwined tail strands with staggered paired heads, curved necks, mirrored halves and a central head-free zone. Tail geometry remains fixed across animation frames.

The enlarged single-head fragment uses the same player/progress as the main sarcomere. Two explicit teaching cycles each have four phases: ATP binding/detachment; ATP hydrolysis and recovery stroke with ADP + Pi retained; actin attachment/Pi release and power stroke; ADP release. Sliding occurs only during the power-stroke phase. The deterministic counter increments at hydrolysis once per represented cycle (0 → 1 → 2), stays unchanged through the power stroke and ADP release, follows scrubbing and resets with the trainer. The displayed ledger contains two ATP molecules and their ADP/Pi products. It is explicitly a single-head teaching count, not total muscle consumption. Head playback and microstepping share the existing player, including pause on leaving the screen.

Mechanistic cross-check: [Molecular Motors, Molecular Biology of the Cell](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK26888/), [Myosin isoforms and the mechanochemical cross-bridge cycle](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26792327/), and Kuznetsov & Mushkambarov (2016), pp. 188–190. ATP hydrolysis/recovery precedes force generation; phosphate release is associated with the power stroke. Visual dimensions, timing and paired-head phase relationships are schematic.

Validation: `node --max-old-space-size=4096 --test histology-test.mjs` (16 tests), including nucleotide stoichiometry, no sliding during hydrolysis, sliding during power stroke, counter scrubbing/reset, head pairs/bare zone and fixed tail geometry. Browser review: desktop, 390 px mobile, full playback ending with two consumed ATP.

## Redesign of smooth-muscle contraction (2026-10-25)

Replaced the sparse drawing with a spindle-shaped whole cell, one central nucleus, eight cytoplasmic dense bodies, membrane anchoring sites and an oblique actin–myosin network. A dashed resting contour and a length bracket make shortening/thickening visible. A separate enlarged network segment shares the original play/pause/step/scrub/reset clock and keeps actin/myosin lengths fixed while anchors approach. ## Osteon and ionotropic synapse (2026-10-28)

User-requested sources were read from the Desktop folders «биология » and «физа»:

- `биология /embryogenesis_tissues.pdf`, PDF p. 6 (compact bone, osteon, osteoblast/osteocyte/osteoclast distinction) and p. 10 (chemical synapse and ordered Ca²⁺-dependent transmission).
- `биология /Teylor_Grin_Staut_2_tom_2013.pdf`, PDF p. 289, printed p. 288, chapter 17, “Механизм синаптической передачи” and beginning of “Роль синапсов”: calcium influx, vesicular exocytosis, acetylcholine diffusion, receptor gating, sodium/potassium permeability, acetylcholinesterase and choline recycling.
- `физа/ВСЯ ФИЗИОЛОГИЯ МЕДВУЗА.pdf`, PDF pp. 13–14: presynaptic calcium entry, transmitter release, local graded EPSP and summation. Only the consistent core mechanism was used: the source's “0.2–1 мВ” delay units and older speculative molecular steps were not reproduced.

The osteon is an original transverse SVG with concentric mineralized lamellae, a central vascular/nerve canal, osteocyte lacunae, canaliculi and a cement-line boundary. Supporting text explains canalicular exchange, Volkmann canals, interstitial lamellae and the distinction from spongy trabeculae. Existing histology reference: Kuznetsov/Mushkambarov, topic 10. The schematic uses illustrative sizes and cell counts.

The nervous-family overview contains a persistent SVG synapse, with six checkpoints and smooth movement of calcium, a vesicle, acetylcholine and opposing Na⁺/K⁺ flux. The chosen example is an excitatory nicotinic cholinergic synapse; it is explicitly not presented as the universal transmitter/receptor combination. Calcium channels are voltage-gated, while the postsynaptic receptor itself is ligand-gated. A local EPSP does not automatically imply a new action potential. Optional depth distinguishes inhibitory ionotropic receptors and metabotropic transmission. Acetylcholine hydrolysis is distinguished from choline uptake. Play/pause, stage buttons, slider, speed selection, reset and replay share one clock; leaving the family, lesson or document stops playback. Timing is slowed for teaching and is not a kinetic simulation.
